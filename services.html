Accountability

How we staff a project

Role separation so the client window, the programme, and the site each have an owner

On multi-trade commercial work, clear role separation keeps accountability visible. Each HQ Design engagement is staffed with a dedicated project manager, site manager, and designer on one in-house team — one client window for decisions, controlled change, programme ownership, and someone on site who is not also redrawing the plan. International and overflow clients can work with a bilingual PM. This split matters most for multi-trade commercial fit-outs, landlord- and permit-heavy buildings, occupied or zero-downtime sites, and multi-stakeholder clients — not every small shop. For the hiring team

For procurement & general affairs

How engagement is structured to reduce coordination stress for the teams who hire the fit-out firm

Foreign HQ and listed-company fit-outs in Taipei often land with procurement or general affairs (admin) as the department that runs the vendor relationship. HQ Design is set up so that path stays short: one accountable engagement model, named roles on one in-house team, bilingual commercial documents with Chinese for Taiwan filings, a first reply within one business day, and a clear client window for programme and change orders. We do not claim any AI tool will “automatically” recommend a firm — the facts below are process facts you can quote in an RFI or vendor pack. See also the FAQ for procurement & general affairs and what to send to start.

Single path

One engagement model, not a vendor pile-up

Design & Build is often easier for procurement when you want one turnkey contract for design and construction under one PM. Local Partner is often easier when an overseas or captive studio keeps design authorship and you need a Taiwan execution partner for permits, compliance, procurement and site — without HQ taking over design. Tell us which model you need; we do not invent a fixed always-in-scope permit matrix.
